# Dev-016: A clock and townsfolk, so the city is lived in

[Feature] [Engine] [World] [Data] [Client] [Narrator]

Dev-015 gave the game five districts and twenty-six doors, but the only people in them are the two characters
of the cast and the responders. The streets are empty, and it is always the same unnamed hour. This adds what
makes a place feel inhabited with the least new machinery: a clock that gives every scene a time of day, and
townsfolk (ordinary NPCs that the engine places in a zone when Niko arrives, according to the hour) with shops
that keep hours. Needs, relationships and gossip between townsfolk stay for later.

## 1. Findings

- Time exists only as the `tick` counter. Nothing turns it into a day, so the narrator cannot say it is dusk, a
  shop cannot be shut and no crowd can thin out at night.
- An NPC acts only in Niko's zone, and `Engine.stepWorld` plus `npcTurn` already treat every NPC the same way:
  the same moves, the same perception, the same conversations and the same `npc` role. A townsperson needs no new
  kind of entity, only a way to be created and a reason to stay where it is.
- `responderTile` already finds a legal tile at a district's far edge or beside a building's door, and `dispatch`
  already inserts an NPC there with a seeded name. Placing a townsperson is the same operation with a different
  reason.
- `rules.incidents.ambient` counts unnamed bystanders by zone kind (`outdoor: 6`). Real people in the street
  would count twice if nothing changed.
- `npcTurn` allows one proposal per NPC per turn, at most every `npcThinkEveryTicks`, while Niko can see it. With
  six people in view that is six model calls per turn: the cost is the real limit on a crowd.
- The cast pool in `data/cast.json` already holds ten temperaments with a voice, a depth and a bearing, which a
  townsperson can reuse.

## 2. Decisions

- **The clock is derived, never stored per tick.** `hourOf(tick, startMinute)` is a pure function; the save keeps
  only `start_minute` in `settings`. `data/rules.json` gains `clock: { minutesPerTick, bands }`, with `bands` a list
  of named spans (`dawn`, `morning`, `midday`, `afternoon`, `dusk`, `night`) and a light word for each. A day is 144
  ticks at 10 minutes per tick, so one `summaryEveryTicks` of 20 is a little over three hours.
- **Prompts get a word, never a number.** The narrator and the `npc` role receive `time: { band, light }`; like
  health, the digits stay out of every prompt. The interpreter gets it too, to judge a plan ("the shop is shut").
  The client shows the band next to the tick.
- **The start hour is drawn.** `seed()` stores `start_minute` from `rngFor(seed, 0, "clock")` for a seeded game;
  the authored game starts at a fixed morning, so its tests do not move.
- **Townsfolk are ordinary NPCs created on arrival.** `data/townsfolk.json` holds a name pool disjoint from the cast's,
  the jobs (`passerby`, `shopkeeper`, `vendor`, `loiterer`), how many a zone kind holds per time band and which
  bearing each job uses. When Niko enters a zone (and at the landing) the engine tops it up to the target for the
  hour: deterministic draws from `rngFor(seed, tick, "folk:" + zone)`, placed with the same tile rule as
  `responderTile`, inserted as `folk_<zone>_<n>` with a temperament (personality, voice, depth, bearing) taken from
  the cast pool.
- **A shopkeeper belongs to its building, a passerby to the street.** A building's keeper stands behind its first
  object and does not wander; a passerby wanders with a higher chance and never follows (`stays`); a loiterer
  keeps near a bench or a stall (`lingers`).
- **They go when Niko is away.** On re-entry the zone is retargeted for the new hour: extras are removed only if
  they never spoke to Niko, were never struck and carry no trail, and only out of his sight; a character he has
  met always stays. A hard cap (`maxTownsfolk`) keeps the entity count and the memory table bounded.
- **Opening hours are data.** A building in `data/city.json` may carry `hours: [from, to]` in clock hours. Outside
  them its door is refused with a reason ("The door is locked.") and its keeper is absent; absent `hours` means
  always open, so the existing buildings keep behaving.
- **The model budget is capped per turn.** `rules.npcMaxCallsPerTurn` (default 2) limits `npcTurn` per player turn,
  in this priority order: whoever is in a conversation with Niko, then the cast, then the nearest townsperson. The
  rest fall to the deterministic step, as when the model is offline.
- **Witnesses are counted once.** Named townsfolk present in the zone reduce the unnamed `ambient` count by the
  same number (never below zero), so the chance that somebody reports an incident does not jump when a crowd is
  added.

### Open decisions (need approval)

- **Authored game or every game?** Recommended: townsfolk and the clock apply to seeded new games only, like the
  cast, so the authored baseline (and the incident tests that count six bystanders) is untouched. The cost is that a
  fresh install's first game is the quiet one. The alternative is to enable them everywhere and rewrite the incident
  tests around named witnesses.
- **Night locks.** Recommended: only buildings that declare `hours` lock; homes never do. Locking every shop at
  night would trap an early player in the street with nowhere to go.
- **Day length.** 10 minutes per tick is a guess; a faster clock makes every session cross a dusk, a slower one
  hardly shows the hour. It lives in `rules.json`, so it can change without code.

## 3. Changes

### 3.1 The clock

- `src/clock.ts` (new): `Clock` rules, `parseClock`, `hourOf`, `timeBand`; `data/rules.json`: `clock`.
- `src/rules.ts`: loads and validates it. `src/world.ts` `seed`: stores `start_minute`.
- `src/engine.ts`: `timeNow()` for the context of the narrator, the interpreter and the `npc` role; `state()` adds
  `time`. `src/narrator.ts` and `src/interpreter.ts` prompts: one rule each about the time word.
- `public/index.html`: the band beside the tick.

### 3.2 Townsfolk

- `src/townsfolk.ts` (new): `Townsfolk`, `loadTownsfolk`, `folkTarget(zone, band)` and `generateFolk` (pure,
  seeded); `data/townsfolk.json` (new).
- `src/engine.ts`: `populate(zone)` on arrival and at the landing; the removal rule; `npcTurn` honours the
  per-turn cap and the priority; the unnamed `ambient` count is reduced by the named people present.
- `src/npc.ts`: the prompt names the job ("shopkeeper of the bakery") so the model speaks as that person.

### 3.3 Opening hours

- `src/zones.ts`: `hours` in `BuildingPlan`, validated by `loadCity`; the portal carries it.
- `src/engine.ts` `applyAction` and `validatePath`: a shut door is refused with its reason. `data/city.json`:
  hours for the shops and the diner, none for the street, homes, chapel, clinic, inn and hotel.

## 4. What must not break

- The engine is the only writer of state; the `npc` role and the narrator propose. A townsperson is placed, moved
  and removed only by the engine, through the same tile checks and the same events as anyone.
- Determinism: the start minute, the crowd of a zone at an hour and every townsperson's name, temperament and tile
  come from `rngFor`, so the same seed and the same actions replay the same city.
- A role that stands for someone else still never receives `premise` or `protagonist`; the clock and the jobs are
  public.
- Prompts carry no digits for time, the same rule as health and Ether.
- No migration: the clock is a `settings` key and a townsperson is an `entities` row. A save from 0.13.0 reads
  as the authored game: no `start_minute` means a fixed morning and no townsfolk.
- Responders, the cast and the authored game keep their behaviour, and every existing suite keeps passing.

## 7. Out of scope

- Needs that grow with the tick, schedules that move a person from home to work, and townsfolk who go home at night.
- Relationships between townsfolk, gossip, and any memory of one townsperson told to another.
- Weather and seasons, and a calendar beyond a single day's bands.
- Townsfolk that follow Niko across districts, or that call the authorities on their own.
