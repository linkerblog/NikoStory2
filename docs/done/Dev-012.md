# Dev-012: Incidents and a generated government that reacts

[Feature] [Engine] [DB] [Data] [World]

Nothing in the world reacts to Niko today: he can drop out of the sky and nobody beyond the two people in
the house ever hears of it. This adds the first consequence loop. The landing becomes an **incident**; the
people who saw it may **report** it; a **government** (a city and its institutions, written by the LLM
once per new game) answers with a **protocol** of delayed steps that put responders on the grid. A
**scheduled-event queue** carries every delay. It is step 2 of the realism roadmap: it proves reaction on
the street and the house that already exist, before the city grows.

## 1. Findings

- The tick is the only clock and there is no delayed effect: everything resolves inside `stepWorld`.
- `Engine.record` already stores each event with its witnesses (vision range plus line of sight), so
  "who could report this" is a query, not a new system.
- NPCs act only in Niko's zone and never cross a door. A responder can therefore be *spawned* through a
  zone's door, exactly where Niko would appear (`entryTile`), but cannot yet chase him across zones.
- The `cast` role is the pattern for "the model writes, the engine validates, a pool is the fallback":
  asked at `reset` with a seed, never on the first authored boot, never blocking a new game.
- `data/world.json` is passed to the narrator and the architect verbatim, so a city added to
  `WorldFacts` reaches both prompts with no new plumbing.
- The scene already says a witness "came to warn Niko that people will come looking for it". The
  government is the other half of that sentence.

## 2. Decisions

- **The government is data in the save, written by a new `government` role.** At `reset(…, { seed })`
  the engine plants a deterministic government drawn from `data/government.json`, then asks the role to
  replace it. The result lives in `settings.government`, so the save replays. The first authored boot asks
  nobody, like `cast`.
- **The model writes the institutions, never the outcome.** A government is a city (invented name and
  summary), 2 to 5 institutions (id, name, title, tone) and a protocol of at most 6 steps. Every number is
  bounded by `rules.incidents.government`, every tag comes from a closed vocabulary, every institution id
  must exist. An invalid step is dropped; a draft with no unconditional first response is rejected whole
  and the fallback stands. Risky outcomes (who reports, how long it takes) are rolled by the engine.
- **Incident, then report, then response.** The landing creates an `incident` with tags derived from what
  happened: `sky_fall` always, `ether` if Niko braced (the discharge is visible to instruments). Each named
  witness other than Niko, plus an *ambient* number of unnamed bystanders for the zone kind
  (`rules.incidents.ambient`), rolls `rngFor(seed, tick, "report:<incident>:<n>")` against `reportChance`.
  Any success schedules a `report` after a delay drawn from `reportDelay`; none leaves the incident
  `unreported` for good, so a quiet landing is possible.
- **Steps are relative to the first report.** When the report fires, every protocol step is queued at
  `tick + after`. At fire time a step is skipped unless its `when` (`always`, `contacted`, `uncontacted`)
  and its `requires` tags hold. A firing step shows its `cue` (what Niko can sense, such as sirens) and
  spawns `units` responders.
- **Responders are ordinary NPCs.** They are `npc` entities with an `approach niko` agenda, a title and a
  surname from `data/government.json`, `personality` from the institution and `voice` from its tone. They
  move, perceive, talk and are struck through the same rules as everyone else. They appear through the
  zone's door (or the far edge of an outdoor zone), never inside a wall.
- **The institution tracks Niko only through sightings.** The incident keeps `last_zone`. When Niko enters
  a zone while a report is live, the same roll runs for that zone and a success queues a `sighting` that
  moves `last_zone`. Responders go to `last_zone`, so slipping into a building can lose them, and a
  responder who sees Niko is a certain sighting.
- **Contact is talking.** The first `talk` to a responder marks the incident `contacted` and adds the
  `unregistered` tag: the responder asked for papers and Niko has none.
- **Time is compressed.** A tick is one beat of play, not a second, so protocol delays are tens of ticks.
  They are provisional numbers in the fallback and the model's draft is bounded by `minAfter`/`maxAfter`.
- **A new role.** `government` joins `LLM_ROLES`, with its own model, reasoning flag (on by default, one
  call per new game) and idle timeout, logged to `llm_calls` under its real role.

## 3. Scope

### 3.1 Data

- `data/government.json`: the tag vocabulary, pools (cities, surnames), and the fallback institutions
  and protocol. A `{city}` placeholder in names is filled from the drawn city.
- `data/rules.json`: `incidents` with `reportChance`, `reportDelay`, `ambient` by zone kind and the
  `government` bounds (`maxInstitutions`, `maxSteps`, `maxUnits`, `minAfter`, `maxAfter`, `maxText`).

### 3.2 Modules

- `src/government.ts`: types, `loadGovernmentData`, `generateGovernment(data, seed)`,
  `parseGovernmentDraft`, `governmentPrompt`, `GovernmentWriter`, `OpenRouterGovernor`.
- `src/incidents.ts`: `IncidentStore` (rows of `incidents` and `scheduled_events`) and the pure helpers
  (`incidentTags`, `stepIsDue`). The engine is its only caller.
- `src/db.ts`: migration 9 (`incidents`, `scheduled_events`); `clearSave` wipes both.
- `src/rules.ts`: parse and default `incidents`.
- `src/engine.ts`: `record` returns the event id; `land` raises the incident; `stepWorld` runs the queue;
  `enterPortal` rolls sightings; the first `talk` marks contact; `debug()` lists incidents and the queue;
  `narrate` adds the city to the world facts.
- `src/world.ts`: `WorldFacts.city` (optional).
- `src/llm.ts`, `src/models.ts`, `src/server.ts`, `src/game.ts`: the new role, wired like `cast`.
- Docs: `README.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `.env.example`.

## 4. What must not break

- The engine is the only writer: the model returns a draft, the engine validates and stores it; units are
  spawned and moved by the engine through the same tile checks.
- Determinism: every roll is `rngFor(seed, tick, key)`; the queue is ordered by `(due_tick, id)`; no
  `Math.random` and no wall clock.
- Reactions come from knowledge: a report needs a witness or a bystander, and the institution learns
  Niko's place only through a sighting.
- Migrations are append-only; an old save without a government simply raises no incident.
- A missing, invalid, failing or over-budget `government` call changes nothing: the fallback stands.
- The existing suites keep passing without edits unless an assertion truly encoded "nobody ever comes".

## 7. Out of scope

- Custody, arrest, search of other zones, pursuit across doors, and any consequence beyond a responder
  walking up to Niko and talking.
- Incidents other than the arrival (a blow in the street is the next obvious one).
- NPCs knowing the alert (rumours, `facts_known` for NPCs), the press, and a city larger than the street.
- Money, identity papers and needs for Niko.

## 9. Notes from the implementation

- The house has a one-tile gap between its rooms. When a responder stands in it waiting to speak, a second one
  spawned behind it cannot path through (`bfsStep` treats NPCs as walls) and waits in the bedroom. It is
  honest congestion, not a bug in the queue, and a wider street will not have it; the BFS is untouched.
- `game.ts` validates the governor's result again with `sanitizeGovernment`: the engine trusts no role, so a
  writer that skips its own check cannot plant a bad government.
- The Debug tab gained an `[ INCIDENTS ]` and a `[ QUEUE ]` block (read-only; the client still never simulates).

## TL;DR

The landing becomes an incident that witnesses may report; a government written by the LLM once per new
game answers through a delayed, conditional protocol of responders who arrive by the same rules as any
NPC, carried by a deterministic scheduled-event queue.
